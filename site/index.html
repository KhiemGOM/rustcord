A Discord client that idles at 9 MB.

Rustcord is an open-source Discord client for Windows, written in Rust and drawn with Direct3D 11. It's one 32.7 MB exe with chat, voice, Go Live and end-to-end encrypted calls.

Discord is rewriting its client in Rust too.

Project Phoenix is Discord's own rewrite, built on Rust and Zed's GPUI. A preview showed it at about 290 MB in Task Manager with two accounts signed in. There is no public beta or release date yet.

When Phoenix ships it will be official and cross-platform. Rustcord runs on Windows today, and its source is open for anyone to read and build.

Before you download

Can my account get banned?
It can. Third-party clients are against Discord's Terms of Service, and Discord can suspend or ban accounts that use one. Rustcord identifies itself as the official desktop client so features work. Use it at your own risk, and consider a secondary account.
Why does Windows warn me when I open it?
The exe isn't code-signed, so SmartScreen warns on first launch. Click More info, then Run anyway. CI builds each release from a signed tag and publishes rustcord.exe.sha256 beside it.
What do I need?
Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. Activities and video embeds use WebView2, which Windows 11 and current Windows 10 already have. Download the exe or the zip and run it.
Do I keep my login when I update?
Yes. Your login and cached servers live in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Rustcord. Replace the exe and they carry over.
Is it open source?
Yes, under MIT or Apache-2.0. It doesn't bundle Discord's icons, fonts or sounds. Like Discord's web client, it fetches them from discord.com on first launch.
Why only Windows?
It draws with Direct3D 11, plays audio through WASAPI and shows embeds with WebView2. Building on Windows directly is how it stays one small exe.

32.7 MB. Nothing to install.
