Discord doesn't need a gig of RAM.

Rustcord is a native Discord client for Windows, built in Rust on Direct3D 11. No Electron, no installer, no six background processes. Just one 32.7 MB exe that idles at 9 MB and still does voice, Go Live, webcams, and encrypted calls.

Open before Discord gets off its splash screen.

Not a text-only toy.

Layouts, colour tokens and keyboard shortcuts are matched against live captures of Discord. Voice, screen sharing, DAVE encryption, forums, Nitro themes, and all 24 settings pages are built in. Nothing to relearn, and nothing missing when you hop into a call.

Yes, we saw Project Phoenix.

Discord is rewriting its own desktop client in Rust on Zed's GPUI. Their CTO showed a Task Manager preview around 290 MB with two accounts signed in, with no public beta and no release date.

When Phoenix ships, it will be official and cross-platform. Until then, Electron is still what sits in your taskbar. Rustcord is a 32.7 MB open-source build you can read, compile, and run on Windows today.

Read this before you run it.

Can this get my Discord account banned?
Yes. Third-party clients violate Discord's Terms of Service, and Discord can suspend or ban accounts that use them. Rustcord identifies itself as the official desktop client so features work normally. Use it at your own risk, and test on an alt if your main account matters to you.
Why does Windows SmartScreen warn me?
The exe isn't code-signed, so Windows flags it on first launch. Click More info, then Run anyway, or build it yourself with cargo run --release. Every release binary is compiled by GitHub Actions from a signed tag alongside rustcord.exe.sha256.
Wait — is it really 9 MB, or is Task Manager hiding something?
9 MB is Task Manager's working set. Private commit is right next to it in the table above: 60 MB at idle versus Discord's 966 MB, and 116 MB versus 1,879 MB after clicking through 21 servers. Images decode at the exact size they're drawn and get freed once they scroll off screen instead of piling up in a hidden browser.
Why only Windows?
Rustcord talks straight to Windows: Direct3D 11 for rendering, WASAPI for audio, and the system WebView2 for Activities and video embeds. Skipping a cross-platform browser runtime is how it stays 32.7 MB and starts in 0.66 s.
What do I need, and do updates wipe my login?
Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit). WebView2 is already preinstalled on Windows 11 and current Windows 10. Download the exe or zip and run it. Your login and server cache stay in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Rustcord, so updating just means swapping the exe.
Is it open source? Where do Discord's icons and sounds come from?
Dual-licensed under MIT and Apache-2.0. The repo and binary bundle zero Discord icons, fonts, or sounds. Just like Discord's web client, Rustcord fetches them from discord.com on first launch.
